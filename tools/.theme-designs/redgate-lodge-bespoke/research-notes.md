# Research notes — redgate-lodge-bespoke (2026-07-29)

Time-boxed pass: 4 searches (dealer design best-in-class, luxury hospitality
web/typography, car-supermarket UX, trust-signal display patterns). Findings
distilled to principles; no layout copied.

1. **Luxury hospitality sites pair one elegant serif for display with one calm
   sans for everything else, and stop at two families.** → ADOPT: EB Garamond
   (display) + Lato (body/UI), nothing else. The logo's letterspaced serif
   caps make the pairing feel inevitable rather than decorative.
2. **Country-house/hotel sites signal premium through restraint: ivory pages,
   hairline rules, generous whitespace — not through dark themes or gold
   gradients.** → ADOPT as the surface grammar. The claret is a ribbon accent
   on ivory, not a wall color. Explicitly NOT adopting gold hex anywhere —
   the gold lives only in the logo image.
3. **Typography should complement photography, never fight it.** → ADOPT: hero
   text never sits ON the photo (split hero, contained figure) — kills the
   scrim-contrast problem for both claret and light throwaway palettes.
4. **Trust signals belong above the fold, and third-party awards/certs read
   strongest as a dedicated, named section — not scattered badges.** → ADOPT
   as the signature move: a numbered "proof ledger" band directly under the
   hero (award, review score, stock scale, aftercare) instead of generic
   chip clutter inside the hero.
5. **Aggregate review score + individual quotes together outperform either
   alone.** → ADOPT: reviews section = one large serif featured quote + score
   line + two supporting quotes. NOT adopting star-card carousels (three
   sibling themes already do card grids).
6. **Car supermarket UX: surface the few filters buyers actually use early;
   filters must open/close cleanly on mobile.** → ADOPT: keep the standard
   2-row toolbar contract (count+Filters / search+3 selects); no nested
   accordion sidebar — this is a 300-car supermarket, speed of scan wins.
7. **Persistent path back to stock from every page.** → ADOPT: "Used cars"
   first in nav, sticky nav row, and the claret PX/CTA band repeats on inner
   pages so no route dead-ends.
8. **60–70%+ of buyers browse on phones; older-skewing buyers punish small
   targets and low contrast.** → ADOPT: 16px body minimum, ≥44px targets,
   ledger stacks to full-width rows on mobile, one CTA per screen.
9. **Buyers want lots of vehicle imagery before the spec sheet.** → ADOPT
   full-bleed-mosaic detail pattern (1 main + 2×2 + "+N" pill) so five angles
   are seen before scrolling hits specs.
10. **Hospitality microcopy ("you're welcome any time") differentiates a
    family supermarket from aloof boutique luxury.** → ADOPT warm voice; the
    dealer's real hooks (free hotel for distant buyers, valeting spa, Bosch
    workshop) become recipe-driven copy slots, never hardcoded claims.
11. **Editorial-luxury peers (and sibling theme gilded-drive) use corner-serif
    hero + gold rules + magazine detail.** → DELIBERATELY AVOID all three:
    split framed hero, hairline (border-token) rules, mosaic detail. Claret
    mat frame ≠ gold rule dividers.
12. **Shadow-heavy "premium" card stacks are the current template cliché.** →
    AVOID: zero card shadows; hairline borders carry the hierarchy. Shadows
    only on genuinely floating chrome (sticky mobile bar, open dropdowns).
